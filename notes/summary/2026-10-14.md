# Summary: 2026-10-14

## Changes
- **Bloom Filter and Reservoir Sampling now keep honest ranges when the enemy deletes values.** Both ignore deletions, so they kept counting deleted values, and in play their ranges often missed the live truth (Bloom's held the truth 100, 51 and 29 percent of the time with no, some and many deletions). They now count the deletions they ignore and widen their ranges accordingly. Measured in play, all of their ranges now hold the truth at or above the 95 percent claimed.
- **Reservoir's Frequency and Count ranges stay useful.** A first, blunt widening left them with a low bound of zero. The structures know the share of inserted units that have been deleted, so Reservoir now scales its Frequency estimate to the live share and bounds the ghosts in its Count with a binomial. Its low bound is back: Count's is never zero, and Frequency's is zero in a fraction of answers (12 to 61 percent with deletions) instead of all of them. On the game's own streams, including growing bursts and drifting favorites, the ranges hold the truth 99 to 100 percent of the time.
- **Presence stays conservative.** For Bloom and Reservoir Presence the low bound is still usually 0 under deletions, because how many ghosts land among the targets depends on how the challenge chose them, not on how many ghosts exist. A model of ghosts held the truth in only 57 percent of challenges at light deletions, so it was rejected. The high bound stays informative.
- The decision is recorded in `SPEC.md`.
- **Linear Counting and LogLog got the same treatment.** They count the insertions and deletions they see and lower their low bound by the ghosts that the deleted share allows (never more than the number of deletions). A shared helper does the arithmetic. Measured in play, Linear Counting's range now holds the truth 94 to 100 percent of the time (it was 96, then 0 and 0 with deletions), with a low bound that is never zero. The price is width: its range grows from about 34 values wide with no deletions to about 280 with many.
- **Two things this did not fix at first.** The estimate (the middle of the range) was still the count of values ever seen, so under deletions it sat above the live count (Linear Counting: 11 percent above at medium deletions, 67 percent at high); the next entry fixes that. And LogLog's range still misses on small streams even with no deletions (71 to 85 percent in play), which is its known weakness; the HyperLogLog fusion is its remedy.
- New tests, written first and failing on the old code, plus checks that break the fix on purpose, including tests that run the enemy generator under harsh trait mixes; 546 tests pass and lint is clean.
- **A balance finding for Count.** At the default 8,192 bits, Linear Counting is more accurate than LogLog until about 50,000 distinct values (for example, 0.8% error against 12% at 1,000 values). With the current enemy sizes, only a long, fast-growing Boss fight gets that big, so LogLog is almost never the right choice. Bigger enemy streams or a limit on Linear Counting would fix it; that is for tuning.
- **The Count finding is now measured.** Tests reproduce the crossover at about 50,000 distinct values, and the sim report has a new Count section: how often LogLog beats Linear Counting at equal Space, and the most distinct values seen. In 200 fights per tier, LogLog was closer in 0.7% of Minion Count challenges, about 21% in Elite and about 33% in Boss; the largest stream seen was 1,449, 39,158 and 140,662 distinct values. So Minion and Elite never reach the crossover, and only Boss does. Nothing in the game's numbers was changed.

- **The terminal game exists.** `pnpm cli` plays one fight: it describes the enemy, you deploy structures and type `ready`, the stream arrives, and you answer the challenge with `strike`. Options choose the seed, tier and loadout. The game logic is a separate piece that takes a line and returns text, so whole fights are tested without a terminal. 584 tests pass and lint is clean.

- **The Count estimate now aims at the live count.** It expects some of the values it saw to have been deleted entirely, more so when values appear once and less when they repeat. For Linear Counting it was 11 percent too high at medium deletions and 67 percent at high; it is now 1.3 and 4.1 percent. Ranges are unchanged and still hold. Where a few favorites dominate and heavy deletions happen, it is still 25 to 35 percent high (it was 53 to 62). 596 tests pass and lint is clean.

- **First playtest of the terminal game.** Presence fights play well. Three problems showed up: (1) with the starting structures, a Minion whose weak point is Count (about 29% of Minions) cannot be won by any bot, while the full set wins about 95 to 100% of them, so the starting set lacks a usable counting tool; (2) Frequency fights are lopsided by strategy (the greedy bot wins 82%, the cautious bot 1%), because overshooting the force is punished hard; (3) the game never tells you the true answer after a strike, so you cannot learn how far off you were. Smaller items: `help` lists structures you do not own, and Presence targets print twice.

## Known limitations
- The Count estimate is still high when a few favorites dominate under heavy deletions, and LogLog's range misses on small streams (see above).
- Tier sizes, HP, attack and turn limits are placeholders; Bosses are almost unbeatable without perfect answers.
- The starting loadout has no Count-Min.
- Linear Counting's infinite upper bound has no JSON encoding yet.
- CI has not run yet.
