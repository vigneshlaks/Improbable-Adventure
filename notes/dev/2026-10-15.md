## Frequency tuning
**Diagnosis** (Minion, Frequency forced, 150 seeds, shipped loadout). Greedy strikes Exact Memory's figure. When the shielded value is in Exact Memory's table (128 values at 8,192 bits) the strike is exact and deals the full BASE; when it is not, the strike is blocked. Blocked strikes were 57% of Greedy's Frequency strikes, and mean f/s on the others was 1.00. Frequency is a gamble on what Exact Memory happened to see. With Count-Min Sketch added (5 owned), blocks fell to 3% but the sketch overshoots about 3x on these streams (f/s of 0.29 to 0.40), and with `OVERSHOOT_EXP` 2 that cuts damage to about a ninth: Greedy won 27% against 77% by the gamble.

**Change.** `OVERSHOOT_EXP` 2 to 1 (damage = BASE x f / s). With the shipped loadout nothing moves (it never owns the sketch). With Count-Min the sketch route is as good as the Exact Memory gamble for Greedy and still rewards skill (Prober 38, Cautious 47). Below 1 Frequency becomes nearly trivial with the sketch (Prober and Cautious about 78%, Random up to 16%), so 1 was chosen. Recorded in `SPEC.md` ("Frequency falloff").

**Tests** (written first, failing): `balance.test.ts` pins the value; a sim test has Greedy play 60 Frequency Minions with Count-Min owned and requires more than 55% wins (it measured 32% at exponent 2). Putting 2 back fails it.

**Check on the shipped config** (200 fights per bot, seeds 7000 to 7199): Minion Greedy 88%, Prober 80.5%, Cautious 56.5%, Random 25%; Elite Greedy 63.5%, Prober 33.5%, Cautious 24%, Random 7%. In line with the 14th within seed noise.

**Not changed, and why.**
- The shipped loadout's Frequency is still the Exact Memory gamble (Cautious 3 to 9%, because it strikes Reservoir's wide upper bound). That follows from Reservoir's honest bounds and the loadout, not from a balance number.
- `BLOCK_PENALTY` was not varied; Greedy's wrong-strike share on Minions is 38%, above the spec's 10 to 30% target, and mostly comes from that gamble. Worth revisiting once the sketch is a reward in the run loop (M4).
- Count-Min's own estimate (its minimum row, always at or above the truth) is unchanged.

- **Engine** (`packages/engine/src/run`): `RunState` wraps a `FightState` (JSON-safe, copy on write) with phase `fight | reward | rest | won | lost`, Certainty and its maximum, owned structures, upgrade counts, the offered reward ids, a reward RNG state, and turns used. `RunConfig` is plain data (rules, encounters, rest points, reward pool, upgrade effects), so the engine stays free of content. Names exported at package level: `createRun`, `stepRun`, `legalRunActions`, `observeRun`, `turnsTaken` (`step` and `legalActions` already belong to the fight). `RunAction` adds `chooseReward` and `leaveRest` to the fight's actions without touching `Action`.

- **Decisions** (recorded in `SPEC.md`, "Run loop, step 1"): rest stop before the Elite; Certainty carries; rewards after every fight but the last; owned structures not re-offered; Resolve also raises current Certainty; Recovery applies after each won fight, capped.

## Run loop, step 2: HyperLogLog and the fusion (M4)
- **Estimator choice** (throwaway script, 1,024 registers, 400 to 600 trials per size). The harmonic mean alone is 33% off at 1,000 values and 687% off at 100. With the textbook switch to counting empty registers at 2.5 m, decided on the raw estimate, RMS error is 2.3 to 3.3% everywhere except 4.0% at n = 2,500 (the theory is 3.25%), because the choice jitters around the threshold. Deciding on the empty-register count instead, and sweeping the threshold: worst case over n = 1,800 to 3,800 was 4.26% at 2.3 m, 3.86% at 2.5 m, 3.70% at 2.6 m, 3.63% at 2.7 m, **3.57% at 2.8 m**, 3.60% at 2.9 m, 3.70% at 3.0 m. A hard switch cannot do better since both estimators are about equally noisy there. `HLL_SMALL_RANGE` is 2.8, `HLL_ERROR_COEFFICIENT` 1.04, both in `balance.json` and passed in as structure constants.
- **Structure** (`hyperLogLog.ts`): 5-bit registers like LogLog, alpha constants for 16, 32, 64 and larger register counts, empty-register count below the switch, harmonic mean above it. Range: Linear Counting's error law in the small regime, 1.04 / sqrt(m) otherwise; deletes handled through `liveCountRange` like LogLog and Linear Counting (ghosts range, live-share estimate). Merge is a register-wise maximum. A first version inflated the range between 2.8 m and 3.5 m; coverage with plain sigma was already 0.952 at n = 2,900 (floor 0.929), so that code was deleted.
- **Tests**: 12 for the structure (unit, alpha constants pinned, a width check so the small-stream range stays tight, delete handling on the enemy generator, and the statistical test: RMS within 1.10 x theory away from the switch and 1.15 x around it, mean bias under half a sigma, coverage at the stated confidence, at n = 500, 1,500, 2,000, 2,900, 3,500, 6,000, 12,000), and HyperLogLog was added to the snapshot and factory tests. Mutation checks: all caught after adding the alpha, width and result-already-owned tests (the transition-zone code that survived was removed).
- **Fusion** (`run.ts`, `fusions.json`): `FusionDef`, `RunConfig.fusions`, `RunAction` `fuse`, `RunEvent` `fused`, `RunState.consumed`, `RunObservation.fusions`. At a rest stop `fuse` is legal when all inputs are owned and the result is not; it consumes the inputs, adds the result, and the rest stop continues. Consumed structures are not re-offered. Config validation rejects repeated ids, fewer than two inputs, repeated inputs and a result that is an input. Content: `fusions.json` (validated), `buildRunConfig` passes recipes on and keeps fusion results out of the reward pool. The whole-act test now fuses LogLog and Linear Counting at the rest stop and wins with HyperLogLog owned.
- **Bots** prefer HyperLogLog for Count when owned.
- **Does the fusion help?** Count-forced fights, 150 seeds, Greedy / Prober / Cautious: Minion with Linear Counting 94 / 92 / 90, with LogLog + Linear Counting 93 / 93 / 91, with HyperLogLog 89 / 89 / 89; Elite 77 / 77 / 77, 77 / 77 / 77, 79 / 78 / 79; Boss (40 seeds, Greedy) 0%, 3%, 0% (Bosses are unwinnable at the current numbers, so no signal). At 8,192 bits HyperLogLog has 1,024 registers and is less exact than a bitmap on the small streams Minions send (about 2.4% against 0.8% at 500 values), and the streams rarely reach the sizes where the harmonic mean wins (see the Count duel above). So the fusion currently costs a little at the start and gains almost nothing. That is a balance question for step 4, not a defect in the structure.
