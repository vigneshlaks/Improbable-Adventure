## Delete-aware ranges for Bloom Filter and Reservoir Sampling
**Problem.** A range is a claim about the answer to a challenge, and a challenge asks about values that are live now. Structures that ignore deletes keep flagging or counting deleted values (ghosts), so their ranges missed the live truth. The in-play accuracy report measured it (how often the range held the truth, with no, some and many deletes):

| Structure and question | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bloom, Presence | 100 / 51 / 29% | 100 / 100 / 100% |
| Reservoir, Presence | 100 / 99 / 98% | 100 / 100 / 100% |
| Reservoir, Frequency | 98 / 96 / 90% | 99 / 100 / 100% |
| Reservoir, Count | 99 / 100 / 100% | 100 / 100 / 100% |
| Linear Counting, Count | 96 / 0 / 0% | 94 / 100 / 100% |
| LogLog, Count | 74 / 21 / 0% | 71 / 80 / 82% (see below) |

(One Minion setup with medium variety and skew, one structure with 4,096 bits, about 80 fights per cell.)

**Change.** Each of the two structures now counts the delete events it ignores (`deletes`). Each ghost needs at least one delete, so there are never more ghosts than that count.
- Bloom, Presence: `low = max(0, flagged - collisions - min(d, flagged))`. High is unchanged.
- Reservoir, Presence: the low bound is widened by `min(d, flagged)`. Frequency and Count were first widened by `d` and then made smarter, below.
- With no deletes seen, every range is exactly what it was.
- The counter is saved in snapshots (`deletes`), kept by `clone`, and summed by Bloom's `merge`. It is not charged against Space.
- Decision recorded in `SPEC.md` ("Delete-blind ranges").

**Tests (11 new), written first and run against the old code**
- Unit: the counter, the widening arithmetic at `d` of 0, 1, 2, 3 and 50, never below zero, high bound unchanged, and the counter through clone and merge.
- Coverage on a stream with 40 percent deletes, 1,000 seeded trials each, using the engine's own Presence challenge so the ghosts appear as real decoys: Bloom Presence, and Reservoir Presence, Frequency and Count. On the old code the ranges held the truth in 0.3 to 3.7 percent of trials against a required 93 percent. Each test also checks the scenario really does flag ghosts.
- New helpers in `structures/testUtils.ts`: `churnStream` (a stream with deletes and its exact live record) and `presenceTargets`.
- Mutation checks: allowing one ghost too few in Bloom, forgetting to widen Reservoir's Frequency, and losing the count in a snapshot each fail tests.

**Cost of the first version.** The widening by `d` was conservative. Under any real churn `d` is far larger than the 12 targets, so Bloom's Presence low bound is 0 in 100 percent of answers (the range reads "0 to however many it flagged"), and Reservoir's Presence and Frequency lows are 0 in 100 percent (Count in 38 to 95 percent). The ranges are honest because they say little. The high bound is untouched and is still the bound a player strikes at to avoid being blocked, so Cautious's Frequency strikes are unaffected.

## Smarter widening for Reservoir Frequency and Count
The widening by `d` made the ranges honest but nearly empty. The structure can also count inserts, so it knows `q = d / inserts`, the share of inserted units deleted so far. Treating each unit as deleted independently with chance `q`:
- **Frequency.** The live count is the ever-inserted count thinned by `q`. The estimate is the ever estimate times `(1 - q)`, and the range combines the sampling interval with a binomial in `q`. The miss budget is split: each side of each source (sampling, thinning) gets a quarter. With everything in the sample there is no sampling error, so the thinning gets it all.
- **Count.** A value is a ghost with chance at most `q` (more units make vanishing less likely), so ghosts are at most a binomial in `q`. Only the low bound changes.
- With no deletes seen, nothing changes.

Tests written first and run against the blunt widening (5 failed): hand-checked numbers (10 sevens, half the units deleted: estimate 5, range 2 to 8; 11 distinct values: low 3), nothing live once everything is deleted, the estimate inside its range, and the coverage tests on a 40 percent delete stream now also require the low bound to be above zero in 80 percent of trials. Sixteen further tests run the game's own stream generator across Churn (0.1, 0.3) × Growth (1, 1.35) × Drift (none, heavy) × Variety (256, 16,384), 120 trials each, and require the Frequency and Count ranges to hold the truth in each mix; they passed throughout and guard the honesty of the smarter bound. Mutation checks: ignoring the deleted share, not scaling the estimate, and allowing only a quarter of the ghosts each fail tests.

Measured in play (Reservoir, 4,096 bits, 100 fights per cell, no / some / many deletes):

| Question and stream | In range | Low bound zero |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency, calm | 99 / 100 / 100% | 1 / 12 / 52% (blunt widening: 1 / 100 / 100%) |
| Frequency, growth and drift high | 99 / 100 / 100% | 6 / 34 / 61% |
| Count, calm | 100 / 100 / 100% | 0 / 0 / 0% (blunt widening: 0 / 38 / 95%) |
| Count, growth and drift high | 100 / 100 / 100% | 0 / 0 / 0% |

The estimate's error does not grow with deletes (33 to 46 percent for Frequency; it is large mainly because 4,096 bits is only 128 sample slots). Count's range is wide (hundreds) because its estimator guarantees only a ratio, with or without deletes.

**Why Presence is not made smarter.** A Bloom bound that models each flagged value as a ghost with chance `q` held the truth in 57 percent of challenges at 10 percent Churn (it needs 95) and 100 percent at 30 percent. The challenge builder puts about 3 ghosts among about 9 flagged targets whenever ghosts exist, however few, so the ghost count among the targets depends on how the targets were chosen, not on `q`. Any bound that is honest at both churn levels is the worst-case one, so Bloom and Reservoir Presence keep `min(d, flagged)`. Their Presence ranges are therefore usually `[0, flagged]` under Churn; a player's real signal for Presence is which targets are flagged and which two structures agree on.

## Linear Counting and LogLog
The same deleted-share idea, applied to the two structures that estimate distinct values.
- **Counters.** Each now counts the inserts and the deletes it sees (`inserts`, `deletes`), saved in snapshots, kept by `clone`, and summed by LogLog's `merge`. Not charged against Space.
- **Shared helper.** `structures/ghosts.ts` has `deletedShare(inserts, deletes)` and `liveCountRange(ever, inserts, deletes, confidence)`. The structure gives its range for the values ever inserted at a requested confidence; the helper lowers the low bound by `min(deletes, binomialUpper(ceil(everLow), q, 1 - miss/2))` and leaves the high bound alone. When a delete has been seen it asks the structure for its own range at `1 - miss/2` (97.5 percent) so that the chance of a miss is shared: a quarter to each side of the structure's range, a half to the ghosts. With no deletes seen it passes the confidence straight through, so nothing changes.
- **Tests (46 new), written first.** The helper (share, cap by deletes, budget, never below zero, infinite upper bound kept, estimate inside the range); each structure's counters; the arithmetic of the widened range (Linear Counting: 10 values with 4 units each, half the units deleted, low = 9.94 minus 8 ghosts; LogLog: rebuilt from its own lognormal range); a 25 to 30 percent delete stream with a requirement that the low bound is above zero in 95 percent of trials; and the game's own stream generator across Churn × Growth × Drift (× Variety for Linear Counting), 120 trials each. LogLog's tests use streams of thousands of distinct values against 64 registers, where its own error law holds. On the old code Linear Counting failed every one of these, and LogLog failed the harsher mixes (its range is naturally wide, about plus or minus 30 percent at 64 registers, so light churn was already absorbed).
- **Mutation checks:** removing the cap by deletes, not leaving budget for the ghosts, Linear Counting not counting inserts, and LogLog's merge dropping deletes each fail tests.

Measured in play (4,096 bits, 100 fights per cell, no / some / many deletes):

| Structure and stream | In range | Low bound zero | Mean width | Estimate error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Linear Counting, calm | 94 / 100 / 100% | 0 / 0 / 0% | 34 / 125 / 280 | 1 / 11 / 67% |
| Linear Counting, growth and drift high | 95 / 100 / 100% | 0 / 0 / 0% | 81 / 276 / 625 | 1 / 11 / 67% |
| LogLog, calm | 71 / 80 / 82% | 0 / 0 / 0% | 179 / 273 / 399 | 9 / 22 / 90% |
| LogLog, growth and drift high | 85 / 86 / 85% | 0 / 0 / 0% | 387 / 581 / 852 | 6 / 16 / 81% |

**What this does not fix**
- **The estimate was still the ever-inserted count** (fixed later the same day, see "Count estimate correction"). The range is honest, but its middle sits above the live count under Churn (Linear Counting 11 percent above at Medium Churn, 67 percent at High). Count damage depends on the estimate, so a player striking at the estimate is hurt by deletes. A live-share-corrected estimate would be a separate change.
- **LogLog's small-stream bias** is unrelated to deletes. In this measurement 512 registers meet a stream of a few hundred distinct values, so its range misses even with no deletes (71 and 85 percent). Its improvement under deletes (21 and 0 percent up to about 80) is entirely the ghosts. The HyperLogLog fusion is the designed remedy.

The crossover is about 50,000 distinct values. Distinct values cannot exceed total events, and with the current `TIERS` placeholders (base burst = size / EXPECTED_TURNS, growth^(t-1)) the maximum totals are: Minion about 5,700 events (High Growth, 8 turns), Elite about 44,000 (High Growth, 10 turns), Boss about 244,000 (High Growth, 12 turns). The intended lesson (LogLog for big streams, Linear Counting for small) barely arises.

Options for M4 tuning, not decided: raise tier `size` so streams reach LogLog's range (also makes Growth, Bloom fill and Count-Min error matter more); constrain Linear Counting (e.g. a smaller bitmap cap) so it saturates sooner; or accept it and let the HyperLogLog fusion carry the lesson. The M3 simulation reports should include, per tier, the share of Count fights where LogLog beats Linear Counting at equal Space.

## Count estimate correction
The Count estimate (Linear Counting, LogLog, Reservoir Sampling) was the ever-inserted count, so it sat above the live count under Churn. Now `liveCountRange` and Reservoir's `distinct` multiply it by `1 - g`, with `g = expectedGhostShare(inserts, deletes, ever) = q * exp(-(m - 1)(1 - q))`, `m = max(1, inserts / ever)`. The range is unchanged; the estimate stays clamped inside it.

**Choosing the model.** Throwaway script outside the repo, enemy streams (5 turns, 200 base events, growth 1.35, drift), 60 trials per cell, mean |estimate - live| / live:
- Ghosts as a share of the deleted share varied from 0.01 to 1.0, driven by variety: with 4 million possible values nearly every value appears once and `g = q`; with 256 values most deleted values are re-inserted or have other units.
- Candidates: `1 - q/2` (up to 38% error), `1 - 0.75 q` (up to 19%), singletons `1 - q` (up to 40% when values repeat), and the units-per-value model above. The units-per-value model was best or near best in every cell and never worse than the uncorrected estimate. Examples (uncorrected -> model): 16,384 values, uniform, churn 0.3: 74% -> 0.3%; 4M values, skew 0.8, churn 0.3: 73% -> 0.4%; 256 values, skew 0, churn 0.3: 19% -> 2%.
- **Limit.** With skew 1.2 and churn 0.3, a few favorites carry most units and the tail appears once; a Poisson count per value then underestimates ghosts: 53-62% -> 24-34% error.

**Measured in play** (Linear Counting, 4,096 bits, 200 fights per cell, universe 16,384, skew 0.8, growth 1.15): estimate vs live, signed: none 0.0%, medium +1.3% (was +11.0%), high +4.1% (was +66.5%). Ranges hold 93, 100 and 100 percent.

**Tests.** `ghosts.test.ts` (the share and the new estimate), `liveEstimate.test.ts` (Linear Counting, Reservoir and LogLog on the enemy generator: within 8% where values appear once and half the uncorrected error; within 20% when values repeat; better than uncorrected under heavy skew; unchanged without Churn). Existing assertions that fixed the estimate at the ever-inserted count now state the expected-ghost formula in the test. Mutation checks on the formula, `inserts / ever`, the `ever` argument and the estimate line; the one survivor (removing the `q === 0` guard) is equivalent because the formula already gives 0.

LogLog's estimate in `liveEstimate.test.ts` uses 64 registers: with more registers than values it is in its small-stream bias, which this change does not touch.

**Findings**
- **Count Minions cannot be won with the starting loadout** (0% for every bot), and about 29% of Minions are Count. Exact Memory fills after 128 values, Reservoir's distinct estimate is too coarse to land inside the 25% tolerance, and Bloom cannot count. With the full loadout Count is won 95 to 100% by the greedy-style bots. This is the spec's open question on the starting loadout (SPEC, "Should Exact Memory stay in the starting loadout...") with a sharper answer: the starting kit lacks any usable Count tool.
- **Frequency depends heavily on the bot** (Greedy 82%, Prober 65%, Random 3%, Cautious 1% with the starting kit). With a uniform stream the shielded value appears about twice, and the damage is `BASE * (count / force)^2`, so striking a Reservoir bound (force 12 against a count of 2) deals 0.56 of 40 HP. A fight needs about 5 damage per turn to finish within the limit.
- **No feedback after a strike.** The game does not say what the true count or distinct count was, so the player cannot learn how far off the range was.
- **`help` lists all six structures** even when the loadout owns three.
- **Presence targets print twice** on a challenge turn: once in the narration, once in the appended query screen.
- Presence plays well: a Bloom Filter with most of the Space, `strike flagged`, won a Minion in 3 turns. The prompt, typo suggestions and error messages read clearly.

Not changed. Fixes are the CLI items above and the loadout question; balance tuning stays with M4.
